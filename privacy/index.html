Wishwell privacy policy

For the Wishwell app on Google Play and the Wishwell website

Last updated: November 28, 2026

Wishwell is a wishlist app made by Galileo Solis. This policy covers the free Wishwell app on Google Play and the Wishwell website, which adds AI gift ideas.

The Wishwell app on Google Play

What we collect

The app has no accounts, no ads and no tracking. Up to version 3, it sends nothing about you or your lists anywhere.

Starting with version 4, the app sends anonymous choices to help Wishwell suggest better gifts, the same way the website logs rounds (described below): who the list is for, the age range, lean, gift style, budget, the interests you picked and your quiz answers, and for each idea shown its title, kind of gift, price range and place in the list, and whether you circled, starred, ranked, cut or already have it. Each quiz gets a new random list id. It never sends your name, email, the details you typed, items you added yourself, a device id or your key. A switch under the ideas ("Help improve ideas") turns this off. The first time you open version 4, the app asks once whether to share picks you made before; nothing from before is sent unless you tap Share, and those rounds are marked as shared later.

AI gift ideas with a Holiday Pass key

Starting with version 4, you can type a Holiday Pass key and the email you bought it with into the app to turn on AI gift ideas. Only after you do that, the app also sends these to Wishwell's AI service, which runs on Cloudflare:

  • To check the key: the key, that email and a random device id the app makes. The key and email are checked with Gumroad, which handles the purchase under its own privacy policy.
  • To write ideas: your quiz answers (interests, picks, gift style and budget), any details you typed, and the titles of ideas you kept, cut or have already seen, along with the key, email and device id.
  • To improve the starter ideas: the titles of AI ideas you circled, ranked, cut or marked as already have, with no id attached.

Wishwell uses these the same way as on the website, described below: it does not save the details you typed or any names, it keeps only scrambled fingerprints of the key, the email and the devices (up to 3), never the things themselves, and the idea pool rules below apply. Your lists still stay on your phone.

What stays on your phone

Your quiz answers, gift ideas, ranked list, group names and the items you mark as bought are saved only on your phone, inside the app. Deleting the app deletes them. If you have Android backup turned on, Android may include this app data in your own Google account backup; that is controlled by your phone's settings, not by Wishwell.

The app asks for your birth month and year once to check that you are 13 or older. It does not save them.

Sharing a list

When you tap Copy share link or Copy as text, the app copies your list so you can paste it wherever you choose. A share link carries the list inside the link itself, after the # sign, and opens on the Wishwell website. The part after the # is not sent to the website's server. Only the people you send it to can see it.

Buy links

Buy links open a store's search page in your web browser. What happens there is covered by that store's own privacy policy.

The Wishwell website

Your lists

On the website, your quiz answers and lists are saved in your browser only, the same way as in the app. Clearing your browser data deletes them.

AI gift ideas

When you ask for AI ideas, the website sends your quiz answers to Wishwell's AI service, which runs on Cloudflare and uses Cloudflare's AI models to write the ideas. That includes the interests and quiz picks you chose, the gift style and budget, any details you typed, and the titles of ideas you kept, cut or have already seen. Wishwell uses these to write your ideas and does not save the details you typed or any names.

Page counts

To see which pages people visit, the website counts each page view and clicks on a few buttons (Try it free, the Holiday Pass key buttons, the Google Play badge, a waitlist sign-up and the coupon codes link). Each count is added to an hourly total for that page (the hour in US Eastern time, never the exact time), along with the domain of the site that linked you there, if any, and whether you are on a phone or a computer. Wishwell keeps only those hourly totals. The counting uses no cookies and no id, and it does not keep your internet address or the full address of the page you came from (your address is used for a moment to stop floods of fake counts, then dropped). Page views follow the same sharing choice as the round log below: after No thanks nothing is counted, and if your browser asks sites not to track you (Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track), nothing is counted unless you tap Count me in. When someone taps Count me in, that tap is counted too, as one more hourly total. The Android app does not count anything.

Launch waitlist

If you type your email into "Notify me at launch", Wishwell saves that email and the day you added it, and nothing else. It is used only to send you one email when the Wishwell app and the Holiday Pass launch, and is never sold or shared. To be removed from the list, ask through the contact at the bottom of this page, or reply to the launch email, and it will be deleted.

Free trial and Holiday Pass

Everyone gets 6 free AI rounds a day (people in the invite-only beta get 30 in total). To make sure each person gets only their share, the website gives your browser a random id, and Wishwell's AI service counts rounds against a scrambled fingerprint of that id. It keeps the fingerprint and a round count, never the id itself. These counts are kept for about a week, then deleted (beta counts for up to 400 days). People on the same home Wi-Fi each get their own rounds. Your internet address is not used for this, unless one network uses far more than its share; then free rounds from that network may be capped for a while, counted against a scrambled fingerprint of the address. Older address counts from before this change are kept for up to 30 days, then deleted. Free rounds for everyone together also have a daily limit, so on a very busy day the site may show starter ideas until the next day. If you buy a Holiday Pass, its license key and the email you bought it with are checked with Gumroad, which handles the purchase under its own privacy policy. Wishwell keeps only scrambled fingerprints of the key, that email and the devices the key is used on (up to 3), plus how many rounds the key used each day and week. It never keeps the key, the email or the device ids themselves.

Idea pool: making the starter ideas better

Wishwell keeps the gift ideas the AI writes, so the best ones can become starter ideas in the app and on the website. For each idea it keeps the idea's title, kind of gift, price range, short reason and store search words; the interests, budget and gift style that led to it; and how many times people circled it, ranked it in their top 3, cut it or marked it as one they already have. The website reports those for AI ideas only, by title, with no id attached.

Wishwell also uses these counts to steer new AI rounds: the AI is shown a few idea titles that people with similar interests kept, and a few that people often cut, so it can suggest more of the first kind and fewer of the second. Ideas that nearly everyone cuts stop being suggested. Wishwell also keeps daily totals of how many AI ideas were shown, kept, ranked, cut or marked as already have, with no titles or ids, to see whether ideas are getting better.

The idea pool never holds your name, the details you typed, your internet address, your browser id or your license key, and it cannot be traced back to you. Ideas that mention a name from your typed details are not kept. Items you add yourself are never kept.

Choice log: training better suggestions

To teach Wishwell which ideas fit which people, the website logs each round of ideas anonymously. Each quiz gets a new random list id, made fresh in your browser and not linked to your browser id, license key or anything else. For each round Wishwell logs that list id, the day and hour in US Eastern time, who the list is for, the age range, lean, gift style, budget and interests you picked, your quiz answers, whether you typed details (yes or no, never what you typed), and whether the ideas came from the AI or the starter list. For each idea shown it logs the idea's title, kind of gift, price range and place in the list, and later what you did with it: circled, ranked (and where), cut or already have. Items you add yourself are never logged. The log never holds your name, the details you typed, your internet address, your browser id or your license key. The site asks you once, in a pop-up, whether to share anonymous picks to help improve Wishwell. Count me in turns sharing on and also sends the picks already on your list, marked as shared later. No thanks turns sharing off: from then on nothing is logged and no page views are counted. Until you answer, rounds are logged as described here, unless your browser asks sites not to track you (Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track), in which case nothing is logged until you tap Count me in. Your choice is kept in this browser, and you can change it anytime with the "Share anonymous picks" switch on the Share tab, or by tapping Share my past picks there, which turns sharing on.

When you circle an idea, Wishwell may add a similar starter idea next to it, marked "More like" that idea, and when you cut one, similar ideas move down your list. This happens on your device. Over time, the anonymous log is used to work out which kinds of ideas people keep, and the website uses those totals to rank its starter ideas.

On-phone matching (beta)

In the invite-only beta, your phone or computer picks most ideas itself. It downloads Wishwell's idea pool and a small text model from this website, then compares the ideas with your interests, quiz answers and the details you typed, all on your device. The details you typed are not sent for this. On a device too slow for the model, Wishwell's AI service ranks the pool instead: your device sends the age range, interests, quiz answers and budget you picked, and the titles of ideas you've seen, circled or cut, and gets ideas back. The service doesn't keep any of that.

If sharing anonymous picks is on, the beta also sends what it noticed, so matching can be fixed in updates: pairs of idea titles that look like the same gift; up to a few single words from your interests or details that no idea matched, such as "weather"; and which word picked which idea and whether you kept or cut it. A word you typed in lowercase is sent as it is. A capitalized word is sent only as a scrambled fingerprint, which the service keeps only when it matches a known brand word, so names are never kept. Wishwell keeps these only as counts by word and idea title, with the day, and nothing that links them to you, a list or a device. Words that no idea matched may be used to have the AI write new ideas for the pool. With sharing off, none of this is sent.

Swipe links (beta)

When you make a list for someone else, you can send them a swipe link. Making one sends Wishwell's AI service up to 20 idea titles and their kinds of gift: some you circled and some from the idea pool. Items you added yourself and ideas that mention a name from your details are left out. The person you send it to opens the link with no sign-up, says when they were born (checked on their device and not sent; under 13 can't continue), and taps Like or Pass on each idea. Only those likes and passes are saved with the link, plus a running count of likes and passes for each idea title. Your list then leans toward what they liked, without showing you which cards they liked. A link stops working after two weeks. Anyone who has the link can open it, so send it only to the person it's for.

Holiday Games

The Holiday Games keep your levels and scores only on your device. If you type a Holiday Pass or Games Pass key into the games, the key and the email you bought it with are checked with Gumroad through Wishwell's AI service, the same way as above.

Shopping tools

People in the invite-only beta can add items from a photo or screenshot. The photo is made smaller on your device, then sent to Wishwell's AI service, which uses a Cloudflare AI model to read the items in it. Wishwell does not save the photo; only the item names it finds come back to your list, along with a small copy of the photo kept on your device for that item. Each photo uses one of your AI rounds, counted the same way as above.

Compare prices (beta) and Magic Coupons (everyone) work on your device. Coupon codes you save, and your free-shipping setting, stay in your browser unless you choose to share a code. Store logos load from each store's own website, or from Google's icon service when a store's doesn't load, so that site can see your internet address the way any website can. When you tap a store, Google Shopping, RetailMeNot or Slickdeals, you go to that site, and its own privacy policy applies.

Shared coupon codes

In Magic Coupons, or on the shopper coupon codes page, you can share a code you found with other shoppers. A shared code is public: anyone using Wishwell can see it. Wishwell keeps only the store, the code, what it's for and its end date if you add them, the day it was added, and how many people said it worked, didn't work or reported it. It doesn't keep who shared it or who voted. Shared codes can't contain links, email addresses or phone numbers, so please don't put anything personal in them.

To limit spam and allow one vote per code, Wishwell's AI service counts shares and votes against a scrambled fingerprint of your browser's random id, never the id itself. Daily counts are deleted after 2 days, and the record of which way you voted on a code after 90 days. Your internet address is not used for this unless one network floods the list, in which case shares from that network may be limited for a day using a scrambled fingerprint of the address. Codes that expire, keep failing or get reported are hidden, and Wishwell can remove any code.

Children

Wishwell is for people 13 and older. The app and the website do not let younger people in and do not knowingly collect anything from children.

Changes

If Wishwell ever starts keeping anything new, this page will say so first, with a new date at the top.
